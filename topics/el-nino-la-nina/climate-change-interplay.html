Will It Affect Climate Change? The Superposition Crisis

Demystifying the critical boundary between natural internal climate variability and anthropogenic greenhouse forcing: why climate change is projected to double extreme ENSO frequency, and how Super El Niños breach planetary tipping points.

1. Internal Variability vs. External Radiative Forcing

A frequent misconception is whether El Niño "causes" climate change. Thermodynamically, they operate on different principles:

  • ▶ ENSO is Internal Heat Redistribution: It sloshes existing oceanic heat between the deep ocean and troposphere.
  • ▶ Anthropogenic Climate Change is Net Planetary Accumulation: Greenhouse gases continuously trap net surplus radiative heat, 90% of which enters the oceans.

The Dangerous 'Superposition' Crisis

An El Niño in 2024 does not occur on 1950s oceans. It sits atop a baseline ocean already +1.3°C warmer, pushing absolute temperatures past lethal ecological tipping points.

3. Planetary Tipping Points Breached

Peer-reviewed research indicates that ongoing greenhouse warming will double the frequency of extreme El Niño and La Niña events by 2100.

1. 4th Global Mass Coral Bleaching

NOAA confirmed the 4th global coral bleaching event in 2024, impacting >70% of planetary reef area across three oceans.

2. Amazon Rainforest Savannization

Extreme drought coupled with deforestation threatens the Amazon's self-watering moisture recycling, risking a bistable collapse into degraded savanna.

3. Terrestrial Carbon Sink Inversion

Tropical forests turn from net carbon sinks into net emitters under Super El Niño heat stress, accelerating the Mauna Loa CO2 growth rate to record levels.

The Domestic Reality

The Anatomy of Indonesia’s PPDS Crisis

Unlike almost any other healthcare system in middle-income and developed nations, Indonesia's specialist training framework treats doctors as fee-paying students while demanding the clinical output of full-time hospital employees.

The Pay-to-Work Economic Trap

In Indonesia, university-based specialist medical education (PPDS) classifies trainees exclusively as 'students' (mahasiswa). Residents must pay substantial tuition (SPP/UKT) ranging from Rp 10 million to over Rp 35 million per semester, alongside institutional development donations (SPI). Yet they work 80 to 110 hours per week in public referral hospitals (RSUP), providing over 70% of frontline inpatient care with zero basic salary, zero overtime, and zero BPJS employment benefits.

Status: Student labor without protections

The Routine 36-Hour 'Jaga Malam'

A standard PPDS schedule frequently involves 'Jaga Malam Lanjut Dinas Pagi': arriving at the hospital by 06:30 for morning report, managing ward rounds and outpatient clinics until 16:00, covering overnight acute emergencies and emergency department admissions until 07:00 next morning, and then continuing directly into elective surgical lists or clinic duty until late afternoon—often reaching 34 to 38 hours of unbroken duty.

Physical: 36h consecutive wakefulness

Senioritas, Extortion & Dr. Aulia Risma (2024)

In August 2024, the tragic suicide of dr. Aulia Risma Lestari, an anesthesiology resident at RSUP Dr. Kariadi / Universitas Diponegoro, unmasked the systemic brutality of the residency hidden curriculum. Ministry of Health (Kemenkes) investigations uncovered systematic extortion of junior residents (tens of millions of rupiah for seniors' food, vehicle rentals, and hotels), weaponized sleep deprivation, verbal degradation, and fear of blacklisting.

Watershed: Kemenkes anti-bullying portal

Policy Transformation Roadmap

The 5-Point Structural Roadmap for Indonesia

Eliminating 36-hour shifts and systemic hazing in Indonesia requires fundamental structural reform at the intersection of the Ministry of Health (Kemenkes), the Ministry of Education (Kemendikbud), and hospital management.

01 Labor Law

Statutory 'Dual Status' Recognition

Amend medical education regulations to legally classify PPDS as both postgraduate learners AND hospital healthcare employees. This brings residents under the protective umbrella of national labor law, giving them rights to maximum work hours, rest intervals, and state-mandated worker insurance.

Legal parity with healthcare workers
02 Rostering Caps

Immediate 16h Shift Ceiling & 60h Weekly Cap

Legally prohibit consecutive 'jaga malam lanjut dinas pagi'. Mandate that no shift exceed 16 continuous hours, with a mandatory 10-hour rest interval between shifts. Cap total weekly duty hours at 60 hours (transitioning toward the international 48-hour standard).

Abolishes the 36-hour marathon
03 Financing

Hospital-Based (RSPPU) Paid Stipends

Accelerate implementation of Hospital-Based Residency (RSPPU) under UU Kesehatan No. 17/2023. Allocate 2–3% of teaching hospital BPJS reimbursement revenue or APBN healthcare subsidies to fund monthly resident stipends (at least 2x regional minimum wage / UMR) and abolish academic tuition fees.

Ends the $0 pay-to-work paradigm
04 Clinical Quality

Mandatory Digital I-PASS Handoff Integration

Overcome teaching hospitals' fear of shift handovers by embedding the standardized I-PASS mnemonic into hospital electronic medical record (SIMRS) systems. Require structured 15-minute multidisciplinary handoffs between day and night float teams, eliminating informal oral shortcuts.

-30% adverse events without long shifts
05 Governance

Independent Anti-Bullying Ombudsman

Permanently separate grievance investigations from department chairs and faculty deans to eliminate whistleblower career retaliation. Grant an independent medical ombudsman the authority to suspend toxic program heads, revoke hospital teaching licenses, and refer financial extortion directly to law enforcement.

Zero tolerance for extortion & abuse
06 Doctor Safety

Hospital-Funded Post-Call Rideshare

Following the US and Australian model, require hospitals to provide rideshare vouchers (e.g. Gojek/Grab Corporate) or dedicated rest quarters for any resident leaving an acute overnight shift, eliminating deadly post-call motorcycle and car accidents on Indonesian roads.

Direct reduction in doctor transit mortality

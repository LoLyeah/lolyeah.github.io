Jakarta / case studies

Policy becomes visible at the interchange.

Jakarta's sequence is not a simple replacement of bridges with crossings. It moves from isolated, car-protecting structures toward a portfolio: surface access where speeds permit, and accessible multi-level hubs where transit systems meet.

01 / Four interventions

Different problems, different structures.

Bundaran HI / 2018

Temporary pelican crossing

The legacy JPO was dismantled ahead of the Asian Games, partly restoring the Selamat Datang Monument sightline. A roughly 13-second pedestrian phase exposed the central conflict: direct access and public-realm dignity versus fear of arterial queues. The later MRT underpass shows that a surface crossing can be a transition, not a final answer.

CSW-ASEAN / 2021

Multi-level TOD hub

The former transfer required a steep 117-step climb from elevated Transjakarta Corridor 13, despite ASEAN MRT being nearby. The five-storey interchange joins BRT and MRT through elevators, escalators, retail, and covered links. Grade separation works here because it solves a network transfer problem, not merely a road-crossing problem.

Kebayoran Lama / 2023

Skywalk as transfer infrastructure

The approximately 450-metre elevated link connects KRL with Transjakarta corridors 8 and 13. CCTV, elevators, escalators, and LED lighting matter as much as the deck. Its value is removing transfers from narrow streets and active rail crossings while preserving a continuous path.

GBK / Senayan / 2019

Revitalized JPOs

Ramps, composite decks, solar power, and lighting seek to answer the accessibility and security critique. The lesson is conditional: a JPO is only universal infrastructure when its access systems are operational, legible, maintained, and safe after dark.

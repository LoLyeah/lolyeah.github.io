Geothermal Energy in Indonesia (PLTP):
Is It Really a Good Choice to Build Many Geothermal Plants?

Indonesia sits upon approximately 40% of the world's volcanic geothermal reserves—over 24 Gigawatts of subterranean thermal potential. Yet after four decades of commercial operation, only ~2.4 to 2.6 GW (~10%) is utilized. Why hasn't Indonesia built hundreds of geothermal plants? Is scaling geothermal the golden key to replacing 35+ GW of dirty coal, or does it face insurmountable barriers in exploration risk, grid overcapacity, conservation forests, and tariff politics?

Section 01 • Tectonic Endowment

The Geological Gift of the Pacific Ring of Fire

Indonesia sits directly atop the collision front where the dense Indo-Australian oceanic plate subducts beneath the continental Eurasian Plate. This continuous tectonic compression creates a 5,000 km active volcanic arc spanning Sumatra, Java, Bali, Nusa Tenggara, North Sulawesi, and Maluku. Magma bodies intrude relatively close to the surface (1.5 to 3.5 km depth), heating subterranean groundwater into high-enthalpy hydrothermal systems exceeding 250°C to 320°C.

The Spatial Mismatch

Java accounts for ~70% of Indonesia's national electricity consumption, but its geothermal reserves are already heavily developed or located in high-density volcanic parks. Meanwhile, Sumatra and Eastern Indonesia hold enormous unexploited steam potential, but lack high-voltage transmission lines or local industrial demand to consume it.

Key Solution: Sumatra-Java HVDC Subsea Interconnection Cable.

Section 03 • Strategic Assessment

Is It Really a Good Choice for Indonesia to Have Many Geothermal Power Plants?

The instinctive answer given by public discourse is almost always "Yes—Indonesia has 40% of the world's potential, so build as much as possible!" However, when viewed through the lenses of power grid stability, capital economics, exploration risk, and forestry regulation, the reality is far more nuanced. Below is the multi-dimensional breakdown of why geothermal is essential, yet fraught with complex real-world friction:

1. Why Geothermal is Invaluable: The Baseload Trap

Indonesia operates ~35,000 MW of coal-fired power plants (PLTU). Under the Net Zero 2060 commitment, these must be decommissioned. Solar PV in tropical Indonesia achieves only a 15–20% capacity factor due to equatorial cloud covers and monsoon seasons. Attempting to replace 35 GW of continuous coal with intermittent solar PV would require unfeasible, astronomical battery storage (BESS) investments. Geothermal is one of the only true zero-carbon sources capable of running 24/7/365 with spinning rotational inertia to maintain grid frequency.

2. The 'Take-or-Pay' Grid Overcapacity Paradox

The Java-Madura-Bali (JAMALI) grid suffered from a severe power oversupply (>40–50% reserve margin) following the massive aggressive rollout of coal plants under the 35,000 MW program. PLN is locked into long-term 'Take-or-Pay' power purchase agreements with private coal IPPs. Under these contracts, PLN must pay for coal electricity regardless of whether it is consumed. Adding gigawatts of new geothermal plants to Java right now would force PLN to bear enormous financial penalties for idle capacity.

3. Exploration Risk & Upfront Equity

Unlike solar panels where performance can be modeled before spending, geothermal exploration is subsurface blind drilling. A single exploratory deep well costs $6 to $10 million. Developers must drill 3 to 5 wells ($30–50M total) before even knowing if commercial steam exists. Indonesian commercial banks are prohibited by prudential lending regulations from financing unproven exploratory drilling. Until proven, 100% of risk must be borne by developers' balance sheet equity.

4. Conservation Forests & Social License

Because volcanoes in Indonesia are also primary water catchment sanctuaries, over 42% of prospective geothermal reserves lie within Protected Forests (Hutan Lindung) or National Parks. Although Law No. 21/2014 uncoupled geothermal from mining (permitting utilization in forest zones via IPPKH permits), public apprehension over water table loss, road clearing, and drilling landslides has caused community protests in fields like Mount Slamet (Baturraden) and Bedugul (Bali).

Section 05 • Policy Verdict

The Strategic Verdict: How Indonesia Can Win With Geothermal

01

Government Drilling De-Risking

Instead of forcing private IPPs or PLN to bear high exploration failure risk, the Ministry of Energy (ESDM) must expand its "Government Drilling" program using sovereign climate funds (PT SMI, World Bank GREM) to confirm 3P steam reserves BEFORE tendering.

02

Sumatra-Java HVDC Supergrid

Building the high-voltage direct current (HVDC) subsea cable across the Sunda Strait is the single most vital transmission project in Southeast Asia. It unlocks Sumatra's 9.6 GW geothermal potential to directly feed Java's massive industrial demand.

03

Binary Co-Generation & Green H₂

Existing plants (Kamojang, Salak, Ulubelu) reject hot brine that still contains 140–180°C heat. Installing secondary binary ORC units can extract 15–20% more power with zero additional well drilling, or directly power clean green hydrogen electrolysis clusters.

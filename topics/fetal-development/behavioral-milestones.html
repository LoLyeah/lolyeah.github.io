Clinical Obstetric Classification & Biophysical Profile

Fetal Behavioral States (1F–4F)
& Manning's Biophysical Profile

In 1982, Dutch obstetrician J.G. Nijhuis revolutionized prenatal medicine by discovering that fetuses exhibit four distinct, organized behavioral states from Week 36 onwards, mirroring neonatal sleep-wake architecture.

State 1F NREM Quiet Sleep

1F: Quiet Sleep (Deep Rest)

  • Eye Movements: Absent (eyes closed and still).
  • Body Movements: Incidental startles only; no continuous gross motion.
  • FHR Pattern: Stable baseline, narrow oscillation bandwidth (< 10 BPM).
  • Clinical Significance: Secretion of growth hormone; duration 20–40 min.
State 2F REM Active Sleep

2F: Active Sleep (REM Dreaming)

  • Eye Movements: Continuous rapid eye saccades.
  • Body Movements: Frequent periodic motor twitches and breathing practice.
  • FHR Pattern: Wide oscillation bandwidth, frequent accelerations with twitches.
  • Clinical Significance: Dominant state (~60-70% of time); synaptic pruning.
State 3F Quiet Awake

3F: Quiet Awake (Alert Observation)

  • Eye Movements: Continual slow eye movements; eyelids wide open.
  • Body Movements: Absent or rare slow stretches; tonic muscular alertness.
  • FHR Pattern: Stable baseline with moderate variability; no accelerations.
  • Clinical Significance: Rare in utero (3-5% of time); sensory processing.
State 4F Active Awake Gymnastics

4F: Active Awake (Acrobatics)

  • Eye Movements: Continual active eye movements.
  • Body Movements: Vigorous continuous body acrobatics, strong kicks, rolls.
  • FHR Pattern: Large unstable baseline shifts, tachycardic accelerations.
  • Clinical Significance: Peaks late evening when mother lies down still.

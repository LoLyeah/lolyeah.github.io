01 / THE SHIFT

From moving cars to moving people.

Jakarta's Sudirman-Thamrin revitalisation, Bundaran HI pelican crossing, and SCBD pedestrian networks signal a gradual move away from vehicle-only priorities. The conflict is visible at every kerb: driver delay versus direct, dignified access.

01 / JPOSeparate

Keeps pedestrians away from vehicles, but stairs and broken lifts can turn safety into exclusion.

02 / PELICANConnect

At-grade signalised crossings are direct and universally legible, but depend on compliance and signal maintenance.

03 / CONTEXTAdapt

Tactile paving, ramps, speed tables, greenery, and PKL management make the street work as a system.

04 / PWKTranslate

Indonesian research covers Kampung Kota, participation, and informal transport. Its public-policy translation remains the gap.

02 / COMPARISON

Three models. One street-level test.

Neither every JPO nor every zebra crossing is automatically good. Compare goals, equity, maintenance, enforcement, aesthetics, and fit with Indonesian modal patterns before choosing.

03 / DECISION TOOL

Choose by risk, not by fashion.

This is an illustrative policy screen, not a traffic-engineering warrant. Increase context risk when speed, road width, or weak compliance make street-level crossing unsafe; increase access priority when vulnerable users and transit demand dominate.

Policy sequence: calm speeds and enforce yielding before labelling at-grade crossings unsafe.
